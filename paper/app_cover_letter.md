# Cover Letter — Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics

**To:** The Editors, *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics*

**Re:** "Pitch–Hue Correspondences: A Quantitative Corpus Analysis of Three
Centuries of Mapping Systems" — submitted as a Research Article

---

Dear Editors,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration in *Attention, Perception, &
Psychophysics*. The paper addresses a long-standing puzzle in cross-modal
perception: why three centuries of independent proposals for which color
corresponds to which musical note produce profoundly irreconcilable
disagreements — and what that disagreement structure can tell us about the
mechanisms of cross-modal correspondence.

**What we did.** We constructed a catalogue of 70 pitch–hue mapping systems
spanning from Chinese *Wuxing* cosmology (4th century BCE) to contemporary
music-visualization applications, standardised all color specifications to
CIELAB via a principled conversion pipeline, and analysed the 25 retained
systems (31 in a sensitivity analysis, all conclusions unchanged) using the
CIEDE2000 perceptual distance metric. The key move is treating the historical
record as data rather than as a problem to be solved: disagreement across
traditions is expected; the question is whether it is *structured*.

**What we found.** It is. Organised into eight reasoning families classified
from source texts before any agreement was computed, the corpus shows that
Pedagogical systems — Boomwhackers, Figurenotes, Sudre's Solresol, developed
independently across centuries — agree 57% above the global baseline (though
this ordering does not survive permutation at current family sizes, *n* = 3–5),
while Cosmological systems (Chinese *Wuxing*, Persian, Indian, Diez 1723) agree
*less* than the pooled corpus. Four theoretical accounts were tested
head-to-head: (1) the **structural account** earns partial credit — semitone
distance predicts hue distance (*r* = .393, permutation *p* = .002) — but
the complementary-hue prediction for the perfect fifth fails (80.7° observed,
180° predicted); (2) the **semantic account** contributes a timbral channel
(*ρ* = .368, *p* = .007) entangled with affect; (3) the **statistical
account** is supported for large-scale ordering (*r* = −.94 between interval
size and between-system variance, *p* = .001); and (4) the **hedonic
account** performs best — Procrustes alignment of cross-modal dimensions in
EPA affective space yields disparity 0.559 (*p* = .027), with the Evaluation
(good–bad) axis as the primary bridge.

**Why APP.** The paper is methodological and corpus-analytic rather than
behavioral, but it is squarely within APP's tradition of quantitative
contributions to cross-modal research. We present a reference-free agreement
framework applicable beyond pitch–hue, a head-to-head comparison of four
accounts using multiple independent operationalisations, and a congruency
framework for neural research that resolves the circularity problem in
existing EEG and fMRI pitch–hue paradigms. No new behavioral data are
collected; the value is the curated catalogue, the analysis framework, and the
theoretical comparison — quantitative targets for the experiments that should
follow.

**Open practices.** Data and analysis code are publicly available at
https://github.com/alexTrod/pitch-hue-correspondences. The analyses were not
preregistered.
